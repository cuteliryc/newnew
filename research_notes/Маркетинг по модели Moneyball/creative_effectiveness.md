## Q1. Share of sales effect attributable to creative (Nielsen, NCSolutions, Kantar): exact numbers and caveats

### Takeaway
The often-quoted "creative = ~47-49% of sales lift" figure comes from a 2017 Nielsen/NCSolutions decomposition of about 500 CPG campaigns. It is the most-cited number for creative's share. It is not a universal constant. The creative share has fallen over time: about 65% in the 2006 Apollo Project, 47-49% in 2017, and reportedly 39% for TV and 26% for digital in a 2023 NCS update. The share also differs by channel and depends heavily on how "creative" vs "brand" vs "media" are defined. The direction is consistent: creative is the largest or second-largest single controllable driver, and marketers underestimate it.

### Cited Findings
- **Nielsen "Five Keys to Advertising Effectiveness" (2017) [MEDIUM][OLD]:** about 500 campaigns. Creative accounted for 47% of sales lift from messaging/quality, plus 2% from ad context (49% total). Reach accounted for 22%, targeting 9%, recency 5%, and brand factors 15%. — [Media in Canada](https://mediaincanada.com/?p=157079); [Nielsen insight 2017](https://www.nielsen.com/es/insights/2017/perspectives-want-a-successful-ad-get-creative/); [SlideShare: Five Keys](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/five-keys-to-advertising-effectivenes/86190114)
- **Common misreading of the 47%:** at least one WARC piece describes it as "creative was the most important contributor in 47% of campaigns", a share of campaigns rather than a share of sales lift. Check which definition a source is using. — [WARC (via search summary)](https://cdn.warc.com/content/article/nissan-enhances-creativity-through-new-structures-and-nuanced-use-of-data/134446)
- **Channel split in the older NCS "Five Keys" analysis (2016-17 data) [OLD]:** for TV, creative contributed 37% vs 50% for media; the digital figure is reported as 56%. The source text is ambiguous about whether 56% is creative's share or media's share for digital. — [Enrich Labs summary of MarketingCharts](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/creative-agency-complete-guide-2026); [MarketingCharts](https://www.marketingcharts.com/television-80662)
- **Trend over time:** the 2006 Apollo Project (Nielsen/P&G-era single-source) put creative at 65% of sales lift [OLD]. The 2023 NCS study (panel plus big-data single source) reportedly found 39% for TV and 26% for digital. The MediaVillage column frames this as "the effect of creative on sales is dwindling". — [MediaVillage](https://www.mediavillage.com/article/the-effect-of-creative-on-sales-is-dwindling-why/amp/)
- **Conflict on the 2023 NCS number:** another secondary source attributes "creative still drove 49% of short-term sales lift" to the same 2023 study. WARC's May 2023 coverage (450 TV and digital campaigns) says creative remains the #1 driver but brand's role has grown. The 39%/26% vs 49% discrepancy is unresolved; the NCS primary report was not located. — [WARC event report 2023](https://warc.com/content/article/event-reports/creative-still-powers-advertisings-sales-impact--but-brand-is-growing-in-importance/151035); [Enrich Labs](https://www.enrichlabs.ai/blog/creative-agency-complete-guide-2026); [MediaVillage](https://www.mediavillage.com/article/the-effect-of-creative-on-sales-is-dwindling-why/amp/)
- **Perception gap (Westwood One survey of 305 brands/agencies):** respondents estimated creative and reach at about 17% of sales each, vs 47% and 22% in Nielsen's figures. Marketers overestimate targeting. — [Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/reach-creative-drive-nearly-70-of-sales-more-than-twice-ad-industry-perception/article_47b93892-ff24-11eb-80de-1beb2f9e01ea.html); [Media in Canada](https://mediaincanada.com/?p=157079)
- **Vendor restatement:** Vidmob cites "Nielsen: creative quality accounted for 65% of digital ad sales lift". This looks like a conflation with the 2006 Apollo figure or a different Nielsen digital study; it is unverified. — [Vidmob](https://vidmob.com/transforming-creativity-into-measurable-business-impact)
- **Creative quality multiplies media weight [VENDOR]:** System1's UK cereals case found that the correlation with market-share growth rose from r=0.25 (extra share of voice alone) to r=0.81 (ESOV × Star Rating). This is one category and self-reported. — [System1 via Selfstorming summary](https://www.selfstorming.com/tools/libraries/wisdom/adding-creative-quality-to-a-share-of-voice-model-roughly-triples)

### Inferences
- Methodological caveats on all "% due to creative" figures:
  - (1) They are variance decompositions of *short-term* sales lift in single-source CPG panels, mostly NCS shopper-card data. They do not cover long-term brand effects or non-CPG categories.
  - (2) The split depends on how "brand" is separated from "creative". Brand rose as creative fell between studies, which suggests partly a re-allocation between categories.
  - (3) Contributions are correlational, not causal.
  - (4) In digital, targeting and creative are confounded, because delivery algorithms choose audiences per creative (see Q6).
- The defensible statement for a Moneyball-style program: creative is consistently the largest or second-largest controllable lever, roughly 25-50% of short-term ad-driven sales variation depending on channel and era. Creative quality appears to *multiply* media weight rather than substitute for it.
- The declining digital share (26%) may reflect algorithmic targeting absorbing variance, or the homogenization of digital creative. The data cannot separate these explanations.

### Gaps
- Could not access the NCS 2023 primary report, so the 39%/26% vs 49% conflict is unresolved.
- No Kantar figure for "share of sales due to creative" was found. Kantar's frequently cited claim, roughly "creative is ~75% of brand impact in digital" or similar, could not be located and is not reported here.
- No category-level breakdowns (e.g., auto, financial services, DTC e-commerce) were found.

## Q2. Emotion vs rational: Binet & Field, System1 (Star/Spike/Fluency), Orlando Wood (Lemon, Look Out)

### Takeaway
IPA Databank analyses by Binet & Field consistently associate emotional and "fame" campaigns with larger long-term business effects, while rational/activation work drives short-term sales. Orlando Wood's System1/IPA work adds that "right-brain" executional features (characters, story, place, dialogue) predict attention and large business effects, and that ads have drifted toward "left-brain" features. The evidence is case-database correlational, and the databases are selected for success: awards entries plus a single vendor's testing panel. The direction is broadly accepted in the industry. The neuroscience framing (left vs right hemisphere) is contested and is best treated as a labeling metaphor for feature clusters.

### Cited Findings
- **Binet & Field core recommendation:** design for long-term effects, and the best route is emotional creative work. Emotional campaigns make media work harder, which can reduce the brand-building spend needed. — [IPA "Greatest Hits of Binet & Field"](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-greatest-hits-of-binet-field/); [WARC summary](https://www.warc.com/content/paywall/article/event-reports/new-lessons-from-binet-and-field---media-in-focus-marketing-effectiveness-in-the-digital-era/en-gb/113293)
- **Creativity and effectiveness (Field, IPA × Gunn Report merge) [MEDIUM]:**
  - Creatively awarded campaigns grew market share about 11× more efficiently than non-awarded ones.
  - More awards were associated with more effectiveness.
  - Awarded campaigns skew emotional: 44% emotional vs 19% rational.
  - Both emotion and fame correlate with effectiveness, and fame shows the strongest link.

  — [WARC "Creativity and effectiveness"](https://www.warc.com/Content/79730b92-4925-4281-bc1a-0f487d42c8c9); [IPA blog](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-greatest-hits-of-binet-field/)
- **Lemon (Orlando Wood, IPA, 2019) [MEDIUM]:** based on about 30 years of TV ads. It argues for a "massive imbalance in favour of left-brain features" in modern advertising, and that the rise of left-brained work coincides with declining creative effectiveness. — [A Slice of Lemon (System1 PDF)](https://cdn2.hubspot.net/hubfs/2235762/Lemon/A%20Slice%20Of%20Lemon.pdf); [LBB on Lemon](https://lbbonline.com/news/ipa-publishes-lemon-a-repair-manual-to-reverse-the-crisis-in-creative-effectiveness)
- **"Achtung!" (IPA Databank with Peter Field and Facebook, 2020) [MEDIUM]:**
  - Covers online video campaigns 2016-2020.
  - Right-brained campaigns generated more very large business effects despite lower extra share of voice and shorter durations.
  - Right-brained features were associated with higher attention; left-brained features disengage viewers.

  — [IPA news](https://ipa.co.uk/news/new-research-reveals-key-to-growing-market-share-with-online-video); [System1 "Three keys that unlock attention"](https://system1group.com/blog/achtung-3-keys)
- **Look Out (Wood, IPA, 2021):** 43 IPA campaigns (137 ads), 2016-2020. Right-brained ads are much more likely to build mental availability and produce lasting business effects. — [Look Out summary (Screenforce PDF)](https://screenforce.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/20220519-pdf-System1_Look-out_A-Glimpse-Inside-Orlando-Wood.pdf); [ThinkTV summary](https://thinktv.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/A-Glimpse-of-Look-Out-egta_Orlando_Wood.pdf)
- **Right- vs left-brain feature taxonomy (directly taggable):**
  - Right-brain features: sense of place, unfolding story/narrative, human elements (characters with agency, dialogue, faces).
  - Left-brain features: on-screen text, abstract or flat visuals, decontextualized products, voiceover-driven, rhythmic/monotone.

  — [Look Out summary](https://thinktv.ca/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/A-Glimpse-of-Look-Out-egta_Orlando_Wood.pdf); [Martechseries](https://martechseries.com/analytics/behavioral-marketing/make-ads-right-brain-long-term-growth/)
- **System1 Star Rating [VENDOR, partially validated]:**
  - A 1-5 scale derived from the intensity and type of emotional response, measured with a panel-based face-trace / "feel" survey.
  - System1 claims 1-Star ≈ 0% long-term brand growth and 5-Star ≈ 3%+ growth potential.
  - System1 states the model was calibrated and validated against IPA Effectiveness Awards submissions.
  - Spike Rating, described as short-term sales/brand-linked impact, is called "predictive" in the 2026 annual report, but its formula was not found.

  — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/system1-launches-new-ad-ratings-in-the-us-and-uk); [System1 2026 Annual Report](https://system1group.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/2026_Annual_Report_and_Accounts.pdf)
- **System1 Fluency:** used in System1's *innovation* testing, as a "speed of acceptance" measure in which a fluent idea feels immediately familiar. It is not part of the ad-rating score. In ad testing, "Fluency" refers to how quickly the brand is recognized/linked (fluent devices). — [Research Live](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/system1-releases-next-gen-predictive-tool/id/5028622); [MRWeb](https://www.mrweb.com/drno/news25124.htm)
- **Neuroscience premise contested:** System1's own podcast raises whether left/right-brain claims are "debunked". The only peer-reviewed brain-dominance study found was old and small (Hanna, Wagle & Kizilbash 1999, print ads). — [System1 podcast ep. 8](https://system1group.com/podcast/episode-8); [Emerald](https://emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/eb047387/full/html)

### Inferences
- Selection bias undermines the evidence base. The IPA Databank consists of award entries, so it contains successful campaigns written up by agencies. System1's validations are self-reported. Emotional vs rational effects are confounded with budget, duration and brand size. Despite this, the finding is directionally consistent across independent databases (IPA, Gunn, System1, and Kantar's emphasis on "meaningful/different/salient").
- For a performance-led (DTC/Meta) advertiser, the practical synthesis is a split:
  - Activation creative (rational, offer, product demo) for short-term conversion metrics.
  - Emotional and right-brain creative for long-term demand.
  - Platform attribution undervalues the latter. Wood's taxonomy is directly convertible into tags (presence of characters, dialogue, place, story arc, on-screen text density) for a regression model.

### Gaps
- Specific Binet & Field profit percentages (e.g., the often-cited "emotional campaigns ~2× more likely to report very large profit gains" from *Marketing in the Era of Accountability*, 2007, and the 60:40 brand:activation split from *The Long and the Short of It*, 2013) could not be verified from accessible primary text in this session. They are widely cited, but the exact numbers should be checked before quoting.
- Exact "Achtung!" percentages (share of right-brain campaigns achieving very large effects) were not retrievable because the LBB page fetch failed.
- No independent (non-System1) peer-reviewed validation of Star Rating → sales was found.

## Q3. Distinctive brand assets and branding (Ehrenberg-Bass, Romaniuk); early/clear branding; mental availability; CEPs

### Takeaway
The Ehrenberg-Bass framework holds that brands grow through mental and physical availability. Ads must be correctly attributed to the brand, using distinctive assets measured by "Fame × Uniqueness" among category buyers, and must link the brand to category entry points (CEPs). Platform studies (Meta, Google ABCD) support showing the brand early and clearly in short video. Evidence quality: the Ehrenberg-Bass measurement frameworks are well documented. Platform early-branding evidence is mostly older (2016-2017) and platform-funded.

### Cited Findings
- **Romaniuk's Fame × Uniqueness grid:**
  - Fame = the % of category buyers linking the asset to the brand.
  - Uniqueness = the brand's share of all asset-brand links vs competitors.
  - Both are surveyed among category buyers.
  - Practitioner summaries cite ≥50% on each as a threshold for a "use or lose" strong asset.
  - Sources: *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (2018) and *How Brands Grow Part 2* (2016).

  — [Pushers framework summary](https://wepushers.com/frameworks/distinctive-brand-assets/); [Boringly Effective](https://boringlyeffective.com/distinctive-assets/); [Jenni Romaniuk](http://www.jenniromaniuk.com/)
- **Marketers misjudge asset strength [MEDIUM]:** an Ehrenberg-Bass study (Brus, Hartnett, Faulkner, Driesener) found marketers' judgements are "rarely accurate". They overestimate fame and underestimate uniqueness, which argues for consumer measurement over intuition. — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/marketers-judgement-strength-brand-assets-rarely-accurate/)
- **Early branding in mobile video (Facebook/Meta, older) [VENDOR][OLD]:**
  - On mobile, 47% of a video's value (ad-recall/awareness/intent) is delivered in the first 3 seconds and 74% in the first 10 seconds (Facebook, 2017).
  - A separate Nielsen study commissioned by Facebook found that people who watched <3 seconds still showed lifts of 47% in ad recall, 32% in awareness and 44% in purchase intent.
  - The two "47%" numbers are often conflated.

  — [Marketing Dive 2017](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-offers-tips-for-converting-tv-ads-to-mobile/448510/); [Marketing Dive: brand lift in <1 sec](https://marketingdive.com/news/brand-lift-happens-in-less-than-1-second-of-video-study-finds/377333)
- **Meta/MetrixLab study of 759 video ads [VENDOR][OLD]:** brand presence in the first seconds significantly improved aided recall. "Traditional narratives" performed worst for brand recall; only 38% of them showed the brand in the first seconds. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/facebooks-published-a-new-study-which-highlights-effective-mobile-video-te/512753)
- **Google ABCD "Branding" pillar:** includes early, frequent, multi-sensory branding (logo, product, audio mention) as one of four pillars (see Q4 for the effect sizes). — [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/advertising-channels/video/youtube-video-ad-creative)
- **Creative-measurement vendors operationalize distinctive-asset presence as a tag** (logo in first N seconds, brand colors, product shown) and score consistency. This is a vendor claim. — [CreativeX blog](https://www.creativex.com/blog/how-to-use-distinctive-brand-assets-to-develop-maintain-brand-consistency)

### Inferences
- Tension with Q2: Wood's right-brain story ads and Meta's "traditional narratives" (story-led) underperformed on early brand recall. Story and early branding are compatible only if the brand is woven into the story through distinctive assets and characters (e.g., a brand mascot as protagonist), rather than a logo at the end. System1's "Fluent device" concept addresses this.
- For tagging: "brand visible in first 2-3s", "logo seconds on screen", "audio brand mention", "number of distinct DBAs present" and "CEP referenced (when/where/with whom/why)" are concrete, auto-detectable features.

### Gaps
- No primary Ehrenberg-Bass quantitative study on early branding → sales/recall was retrieved (e.g., the branding-attribution rate norms in which "~half of ads are misattributed" is often cited). That figure is unverified here.
- Category entry point (CEP) measurement evidence (Romaniuk's *Better Brand Health*, 2022) was not retrieved; there is no quantitative CEP → share link in these notes.
- No 2024-2026 Meta-published early-branding study was found; current Meta guidance is largely restated from 2016-2017 research.

## Q4. Short-form digital/video: hook/first 3s, Google ABCD, Meta best practices, hook/hold rates, wear-out, Andromeda-era diversification

### Takeaway
Google's ABCD (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction) has the best-documented quantitative backing of the platform frameworks. Kantar's AI-modeled analysis of about 11,000 ads put the uplift at about +30% short-term sales likelihood and +17% long-term brand contribution. Both are modeled *predictions*, not measured lifts. Evidence on wear-out suggests real creative wear-out in TV/radio is rare, but digital "fatigue" (rising CPA as frequency climbs in narrow audiences) is a different phenomenon and is poorly studied. Meta's Andromeda-era guidance ("creative diversification is the best lever for finding audiences") is an official Meta position. Most quantitative claims around it are agency/vendor material.

### Cited Findings
- **Google ABCD:** developed with Ipsos, with Nielsen and Kantar as independent reviewers. Kantar scored about 11,000 ads through its Link AI in one month, evaluating about 180 creative features against predicted short- and long-term outcomes. Ads following the ABCDs showed about +30% short-term sales likelihood and +17% long-term brand contribution on average. Kantar's own wording is "predicted". [MEDIUM — modeled, platform-commissioned] — [Kantar case study](https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence); [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/advertising-channels/video/youtube-video-ad-creative)
- **Meta mobile-video principles (older):** front-load value and brand, with 47% of value in the first 3 seconds (see Q3). — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-offers-tips-for-converting-tv-ads-to-mobile/448510/)
- **Hook-window disagreement:** practitioner debate about 3s vs 7s hook windows on Meta. There is no primary evidence either way. — [you.com post](https://for.you.com/posts/meta-ad-hook-rules-clash-over-3-seconds-vs-7-seconds-1b7a4958); [Sovran](https://sovran.ai/blog/first-3-seconds-video-ad)
- **Wear-out evidence (largely TV/radio) [MEDIUM/VENDOR]:**
  - Analytic Partners (2020): of 50,000+ ads, only 14 campaigns had run their full course and showed wear-out when replaced.
  - System1: average Star Rating in month 1 = 2.2, and for ads aired continuously 2+ years also 2.2.
  - RAB/ABX radio: 2 of 25,000 ads showed declines, both at the top of cumulative spend/time.
  - The usual interpretation is that "consumers don't get tired of ads, marketers do". What looks like wear-out is diminishing returns to frequency.

  — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/consumers-tired-ads-marketers/); [Westwood One](https://www.westwoodone.com/?p=26877); [Advertising Week](https://advertisingweek.com/aw360/news/there-is-no-such-thing-as-creative-wearout/20469/)
- **Meta Andromeda and creative diversification:**
  - In a March 2025 blog post, Meta described shifting from niche targeting to creative diversification as the top lever for finding relevant audiences.
  - The concept was mentioned 6 times in Meta's Q2 2025 earnings summary.
  - Andromeda is Meta's ad-retrieval system. Practitioners report it clusters near-duplicate creatives ("creative collapsing") so they compete as one.

  — [Jon Loomer](https://pubcast.jonloomer.com/creative-diversification-is-the-new-targeting/); [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/creative-targeting-ai-driven-delivery.md)
- **Practitioner heuristics [VENDOR/AGENCY, unverified]:**
  - About 70% visual difference is needed for Meta to treat a creative as "new" (Excite Media).
  - Cosmetic changes (headline, background color) are clustered as the same concept (Triple Whale).
  - A claimed +22% ROAS for Advantage+ creative vs manual (attributed to "Meta internal testing").
  - A study of 3,014 e-commerce advertisers reported average ROAS −7% during the Andromeda rollout, with previously top performers hit hardest.

  None were verified against primary sources. — [Excite Media](https://www.excitemedia.com.au/meta-creative-diversification/); [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/creative-targeting-ai-driven-delivery.md); [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/meta-andromeda-update-creative-strategy-2026.md)

### Inferences
- **Hook rate / hold rate metrics:**
  - Hook rate ≈ 3-second video plays ÷ impressions.
  - Hold rate ≈ ThruPlays (or 15s views) ÷ 3-second plays.

  These are practitioner metrics computed from Meta's standard fields. No peer-reviewed or Meta-published evidence links specific thresholds to sales. They are useful as *diagnostic* intermediate metrics, not as outcome proxies.
- **"Fatigue" in performance ads** is plausibly a delivery phenomenon: the algorithm exhausts the highest-propensity users in a creative's matched pocket, and frequency rises among lower-propensity users. It is not necessarily a decline in the creative's persuasive power. The TV/radio "no wear-out" evidence therefore does not transfer straightforwardly to Meta/TikTok.
- In the Andromeda era, creative features effectively *define* the audience reached. This makes naive feature → performance regressions on platform data doubly confounded (see Q6).

### Gaps
- No Meta primary document with quantitative evidence for creative diversification (e.g., a lift test on the number of distinct concepts vs CPA) was found.
- No published, rigorous study of digital creative fatigue curves (CPA vs frequency/age by creative) was found. Vendor blogs exist (e.g., [Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/why-ads-resist-creative-fatigue)), but their method is opaque.
- TikTok's creative research (e.g., "TikTok-first" creative and sound-on findings) was not covered in this session.

## Q5. Creative analytics methodology: feature tagging, regression/ML on features, tools, published results

### Takeaway
The standard pipeline has four steps:
1. Tag each ad's features, manually or with computer vision / LLM taggers (logo timing, faces, text density, story, format, hook type).
2. Join the tags to performance data.
3. Model performance on features (regression, gradient boosting, or hierarchical models).
4. Turn feature effects into briefs and pre-flight scores.

The largest published examples are Kantar's ABCD validation (about 180 features × 11,000 ads, outcome = predicted, not actual) and CreativeX's analysis of about 1M ads (a quality score vs CPCV). Academic work exists mainly on predicting attention/CTR from images. Credible sales-outcome validation of commercial tools is scarce and vendor-produced.

### Cited Findings
- **Kantar × Google ABCD:** about 180 creative features coded across about 11,000 ads, modeled with Link AI against predicted short-term sales and long-term equity. This is the largest disclosed feature-level study, but its outcome variable is itself a model prediction, built on survey pre-tests. — [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence)
- **CreativeX Creative Quality Score [VENDOR, 2021-22][OLD-ish]:** about 1M digital ads (CPG, beauty, alcohol) joined to platform delivery data. Each +10% in Creative Quality Score was associated with −4.8% CPCV on Facebook/Instagram (n=367k, 99% confidence) and −5.7% CPCV on Twitter/YouTube (n=150k). The outcome is cost per completed view, not sales. — [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2021/12/14/welcome-the-golden-age-creative-technology)
- **Vidmob × Objective Platform MMM case [VENDOR]:** one FMCG client. Creative scores were included in a marketing mix model; high-scoring TikTok creatives were associated with +36% brand KPIs and +28% sales. It is a single case and not independently replicated. — [Vidmob PDF](https://vidmob.com/hubfs/Transforming%20Creativity%20into%20Measurable%20Business%20Impact.pdf?hsLang=en); [Vidmob blog: creative data in MMM](https://vidblog.vidmob.com/blog/how-creative-data-tranforms-mmm)
- **Academic, attention prediction:** MSI Working Paper 24-110 (2024), "Predicting Attention to Advertising". It uses automated visual feature extraction plus XGBoost on what it calls the largest ad eye-tracking dataset in the literature, and adds an optimization methodology. The outcome is gaze, not sales. — [MSI Report 24-110](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI_Report_24-110.pdf)
- **Academic/industry, CTR prediction and generation:**
  - "Learning to Create Better Ads" (arXiv 2008.07467) covers generation and ranking approaches for ad-creative refinement from performance data.
  - An ACM paper fuses predictive models with LLMs for actionable creative recommendations (SoWide-ViT for ad-level CTR).

  — [arXiv 2008.07467](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.07467); [ACM 10.1145/3725885](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3725885)
- **Tools landscape:**
  - Motion: creative reporting by tag/concept for Meta/TikTok.
  - Vidmob: CV/AI tagging plus MMM integration.
  - CreativeX: brand guideline/quality compliance scoring.
  - Marpipe: multivariate modular creative testing.
  - Kantar Link AI and System1 Test Your Ad: pre-test predictions.

  Vendor accuracy claims found in blogs ("CNN 90.17% accuracy", "340% conversion lift") lack verifiable sources and should be disregarded. — [Motion](https://motionapp.com/blog/importance-of-creative-performance); [Madgicx (example of unverified claims)](https://madgicx.com/blog/cnn-based-deep-learning-model-for-creative-analysis)
- **Pre-test vs market evidence:** Segwise's 2026 guide on scoring creative (vendor) describes current scoring practice, combining pre-launch predicted scores and in-flight metrics. — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/creative-effectiveness-scoring-guide-2026.md)

### Inferences
Recommended design for an in-house "Moneyball" creative model, synthesized from the sources above:
- **Taxonomy.** Combine:
  - ABCD items (brand in first 5s, faces, pace, CTA).
  - Wood's right/left-brain features.
  - Ehrenberg-Bass items (DBAs present, CEP referenced).
  - Performance-marketing items (hook type, format, UGC vs studio, offer, length, aspect ratio, captions, music).

  Use an LLM/CV tagger with a human-validated subset to measure tag accuracy (inter-rater agreement).
- **Outcome.** Prefer outcomes from randomized tests (Meta A/B split tests, conversion lift, geo tests) or upper-funnel metrics less affected by delivery (thumb-stop, hold), and acknowledge their limits. Avoid using last-click ROAS from ad-level reporting as the target.
- **Model.** Use a hierarchical/mixed model with ad-account, campaign, time and spend controls, with features as fixed effects and partial pooling (see Q6). Use gradient boosting only for prediction, not attribution of feature effects.
- Feature effects are likely to be context-specific (brand, category, platform). The cross-brand norms published by vendors are priors, not truths.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed JMR/JM/Marketing Science paper linking automatically extracted video features to *sales* was retrieved in this session. Such literature is believed to exist (e.g., image-feature and video-content studies in JMR/Marketing Science), but it was not confirmed and is therefore not cited.
- No independent validation of Motion, Marpipe or Vidmob tag-based predictions vs holdout outcomes was found.
- No published accuracy benchmarks for LLM-based ad tagging (vs human coders) were found.

## Q6. Statistical pitfalls: small samples, delivery-algorithm selection effects, Bayesian shrinkage

### Takeaway
The single most important pitfall is "divergent delivery" (Braun & Schwartz, *Journal of Marketing*, 2024/25). On Meta/Google, different creatives are shown to different users by the optimization algorithm, so observed performance differences mix creative effect with audience selection. Platform "A/B tests" and ad-level reporting are not balanced experiments. Ad-level comparisons also suffer from small conversion counts and winner's-curse selection. Hierarchical Bayesian pooling and bandit designs are the established remedies.

### Cited Findings
- **Braun & Schwartz, "Where A-B Testing Goes Wrong: How Divergent Delivery Affects What Online Experiments Cannot (and Can) Tell You About How Customers Respond to Advertising"** (*Journal of Marketing*, DOI 10.1177/00222429241275886; SSRN v. May 2024) [STRONG]:
  - Ad platforms' algorithms deliver each variant to a different mix of users.
  - A "winning" ad may win because it was shown to users more prone to respond, not because it is more persuasive.
  - The authors' concern is presenting platform test results as if from balanced experiments, and the managerial decisions based on them.

  — [AMA press release](https://www.ama.org/press-releases/press-release-from-the-journal-of-marketing-market-researchers-and-online-advertisers-are-a-b-tests-leading-you-astray-a-new-study-says-they-could-be/); [AMA explainer 2025](https://www.ama.org/2025/01/07/can-you-trust-your-ad-data-a-new-study-exposes-a-hidden-flaw-in-a-b-testing-on-digital-ad-platforms/); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/ab_id=3896024); [MSI working paper](https://www.msi.org/working-paper/where-a-b-testing-goes-wrong-how-divergent-delivery-affects-what-online-experiments-cannot-and-can-tell-you-about-how-customers-respond-to-advertising/)
- **WARC coverage:** don't over-generalize from platform A/B tests. — [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/dont-make-too-many-assumptions-based-on-a-b-testing-warns-study/en-gb/10190)
- **Bandits for ad-creative testing:** Comparison Lift (JD.com, AAAI) is an experimentation-as-a-service bandit design for testing ad creatives and audiences. It reduces cost vs fixed-split A/B/n. — [AAAI paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17775/17582)

### Inferences
- **Practical rules for a creative-analytics program:**
  - (1) Use Meta's A/B Test tool / Conversion Lift or geo-split holdouts when a decision depends on a causal creative comparison. Even Meta's A/B tool randomizes *users to ad sets*, but within-cell delivery still optimizes. Braun & Schwartz argue that such tests identify the effect of "creative + its algorithmic targeting", which is a legitimate quantity if that is the decision being made, but not "pure persuasiveness".
  - (2) Treat ad-level ROAS/CPA from observational reporting as heavily confounded by spend allocation. The algorithm gives spend to early winners, so losers have tiny, noisy samples. This is a rich-get-richer feedback loop.
  - (3) Shrink feature and ad estimates toward group means (empirical Bayes or a hierarchical model). With typical DTC conversion counts (tens per ad), raw CPA rankings are dominated by noise. Report posterior intervals, not point "winners".
  - (4) Correct for multiple comparisons and the winner's curse when screening dozens of tags. Validate on out-of-time holdouts.
  - (5) Separate the "creative as audience-finder" effect (an Andromeda-era reality) from persuasive effect, and decide which one the business wants to optimize.
- **The core statistical caveat behind the "% due to creative" figures in Q1:** they come from observational variance decomposition, and the same delivery-confounding logic applies to digital channels.

### Gaps
- No published study quantifying the *magnitude* of divergent-delivery bias on Meta for creative tests (e.g., the % of "winners" that flip under true randomization) was retrieved. Braun & Schwartz's paper includes illustrations, but their effect sizes were not extracted here.
- No primary documentation was retrieved on how Meta's current (2025-2026) A/B test tool handles delivery-optimization within test cells.
- No published industry benchmark was found for the minimum sample (conversions per creative) needed for reliable creative ranking.
